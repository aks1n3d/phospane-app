Roadmap

Where Phospane is going between now and 1.0. Each release has one theme. The months are targets, not promises: a release ships when it works, not when the calendar says so.

0.1Now 0.2Trust 0.3Any network 0.4Everyday work 0.5Play 0.6Everywhere 0.7–0.9Polish 1.0Stable
  1. 0.1

    TodayOut now

    small updates every few days

    Stream another computer's screen, sound, keyboard and mouse, Mac ⇄ Windows in both directions, at around 26 ms over Tailscale.

    • Hardware H.264 and HEVC, sharp-text 4:4:4, several monitors and virtual screens.
    • Encrypted pairing with a PIN; built-in Tailscale for when you're away from home.
    • Clipboard with images, files and folders; the other computer's cursor shapes.
    • Phones, tablets and TVs through Moonlight; gamepads on Windows computers.
    • Five themes, light and dark; automatic updates.

    Coming in the next small updates: Windows downloads and updates, and fixes for whatever early users run into.

  2. 0.2

    Trust & first launchNext

    December 2026

    Make installing, updating and getting help painless before adding more.

    • Mac and Windows released together, every time.
    • Permissions survive updates on Mac: no more allowing Screen Recording and Accessibility again after each update.
    • A first-run checklist: name the computer and allow each permission, with live ticks and buttons that open the right settings.
    • Help inside the app, and messages that link to the fix.
    • “Report a problem”: collects logs into one file you can send. Crash reports only if you opt in.
    • A beta channel for trying new things early.
  3. 0.3

    Any network

    January 2027

    On good networks it's already fast. This release is about hotel Wi-Fi, phone hotspots and long distances.

    • Adaptive quality: the stream eases off when the network struggles instead of freezing.
    • Loss recovery so dropped packets don't turn into stutters.
    • A connection quality light that says why: Wi-Fi interference, a relayed path, or a busy computer.
    • Keeps going when you switch networks or wake from sleep.
  4. 0.4

    Everyday remote work

    February 2027

    Things people do every day with a computer that isn't in front of them.

    • Several viewers at once; one controls, the others watch.
    • Guest access: show your screen with a one-time PIN, view-only, gone after the session.
    • Drag files onto the stream window to put them on the other computer.
    • Wake a sleeping computer from its card.
    • Your microphone on the other computer (Windows first).
    • Windows lock screen and admin prompts you can answer remotely.
    • Session history: who connected and when, kept on your computer.
  5. 0.5

    Play

    March 2027
    • HDR on displays that can show it.
    • 120 Hz and above where both sides support it.
    • AV1 on hardware that has it: better picture at the same bandwidth.
    • Game mode in one switch: game keyboard, lowest delay, frame rate first.
    • Gamepads with a Mac as the shared computer, mapped to keyboard and mouse.
  6. 0.6

    Everywhere

    April 2027
    • Linux as a viewer first; sharing a Linux computer later.
    • Intel Macs and Windows on Arm.
    • A browser viewer to try: connect from Chrome, Edge or Safari without installing anything.
    • Phones and tablets stay on Moonlight, kept working well.
  7. 0.7–0.9

    Harden & polish

    May–June 2027, betas

    No new features: fixes and the work that makes 1.0 deserve the number.

    • A security review of pairing and encryption, and automated testing of everything that reads network data.
    • Translations: Ukrainian, German and Spanish first.
    • Accessibility: every screen works by keyboard and screen reader, in every theme.
    • Speed checks on every build, so it never quietly gets slower.
    • Better docs: FAQ, troubleshooting by symptom, and a privacy page.
  8. 1.0

    Stable

    summer 2027

    1.0 ships when all of this is true, not on a date:

    • Updates install by themselves across releases on Mac and Windows, and Mac permissions survive them.
    • At least 99.5 % of sessions end without a crash (from people who opted in to reports).
    • 30 ms or less at 1080p and 60 fps on a home network or a direct Tailscale connection, and graceful on bad networks.
    • Old and new versions keep talking to each other: no forced updates across 1.x.
    • The security review is done and everything it found is fixed.
    • Every warning you can see has a help page, and a new computer gets from download to sharing on its own.

Free, no account

Phospane isn't signed with paid Apple or Microsoft certificates, so the first launch shows a warning once. First launch help walks you through it.

Want something sooner?

The order follows what hurts people most today. If something here matters to you, or something is missing, open an issue on GitHub.
